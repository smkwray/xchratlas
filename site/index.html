Chromosome X Associations
Most Studies Leave Out

Chromosome X carries hundreds of genes, and variants along it are associated with traits from blood chemistry to cognition. Many large genetic studies still leave chromosome X out. chrXatlas maps where those associations fall, which traits carry them, and which nearby genes have supporting gene-expression evidence.

Overview

Why Chromosome X Is Left Out

Males carry one copy of chromosome X and females carry two, which complicates quality control and statistical modeling in genetic association studies. Because standard pipelines are built for paired chromosomes, many studies drop chromosome X altogether, leaving roughly 800 protein-coding genes out of published results.

What chrXatlas Does

chrXatlas draws on Pan-UK Biobank summary statistics to catalog chromosome X associations across curated trait panels and a broader discovery pool. For each trait, it groups significant signals into distinct regions, lists nearby candidate genes, and checks for gene-expression associations through eQTL Catalogue lookups.

Why It Matters

Chromosome X associations appear across blood chemistry, body measurements, metabolism, inflammation, and behavior. Putting them in one atlas lets you compare signal patterns across trait categories, inspect the candidate genes at each region, or look up a specific gene to see which traits share nearby associations.

Methods

Loci & threshold

Pan-UK Biobank sex-combined (both_sexes) GRCh37 summary statistics are filtered at p < 5 × 10−8 per trait, with no correction across traits. Significant variants within 500 kb are chained into a locus within PAR1, non-PAR, and PAR2 separately; these are distance groups, not LD-independent signals.

Candidate genes

Up to five positional candidate genes per locus are selected from Ensembl GRCh37: genes overlapping the locus, otherwise within 100 kb of the lead variant, otherwise the nearest genes. Genes returned only by eQTL lookups are labeled Follow-up and excluded from trait-level support.

eQTL lookups & support

Lead variants are queried by rsID in a fixed priority list of eQTL Catalogue datasets (using Ensembl GRCh37 rsID recovery when needed, without allele-level matching). Returned records means at least one association was returned. eQTL support means at least one positional candidate gene has eQTL p ≤ 10−5 (nominal, uncorrected, no colocalization or tissue-relevance model).

Evidence score

A 0–100 ranking aid combining Pan-UKB metadata (max independent set, ≥2 QC populations, HQ count ≥50,000), locus count, lead −log10(p), positional gene overlap, eQTL support, and non-PAR presence. Components sum to 115 before capping at 100, so top traits tie (26/48 in Broad Atlas; 19/21 in Biochemistry Deep Dive).

Coverage grades

Grades rate upstream Pan-UKB metadata (not eQTL completeness) by first match. N is HQ cases for binary traits or HQ measured participants for quantitative traits.

A
Max independent set, QC in ≥2 populations, N ≥ 50,000
B
Max independent set with N ≥ 10,000, or QC in ≥2 populations
C
N ≥ 1,000
U
Below A–C thresholds

Scope & sources

Source files are Pan-UKB both_sexes summary statistics (some traits are sex-limited); sex differences, X inactivation, and dosage compensation are not tested or modeled. Pan-UKB samples are mostly of European ancestry, and contributing ancestries vary by trait. Loci whose p-values reached the conversion floor display −log10(p) ≥ 300.
